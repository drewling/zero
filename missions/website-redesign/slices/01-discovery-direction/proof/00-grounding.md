# Grounding, written before the direction roll (2026-09-28T04:40Z)

## Mechanism (one sentence)
On a schedule you choose, or when you run it by hand, zero checks your connected Gmail inboxes, keeps conversations that still need your action, and archives the rest with a dated recovery label (`🗄️ Auto-Archived YYYY-MM-DD`, lib/inbox_zero.py `_dated_label`). The menu bar then shows a short Open loops list, and a run can be restored from Undo.

(Corrected 04:42Z per main-lead: not "once a day", not "every thread". Visitor copy must not use those absolutes.)

## Audience scene
A Mac user with one or more Gmail accounts that have silted up: several hundred unread, a real reply buried under receipts and cold outreach. Morning, at a desk, coffee, before the day's first meeting. Skeptical of letting an AI touch mail.

## Cultural home
Mac utilities (menu-bar apps, Things, Raycast), Gmail labels and All Mail, office mail rituals: the morning post, the in-tray, the "while you were out" slip.

## What this first surface must prove
1. What it does, in one line (keeps what needs you, archives the rest).
2. That it is safe: reversible, nothing deleted, you can see and restore each day.
3. The real costs and caveats: macOS 26+, Apple Silicon, Gmail, Jev key billed by TypeSafe, thread text sent to Jev, not notarized, Google unverified-app warning.
4. How to install (one command, readable script).

## Seven grounded candidates, ordered by resonance
1. **The dated rubber stamp and recovery label (office date stamp / postmark).** zero's reversibility literally is a dated label; a stamp grammar (date, account, "set aside", "kept") makes the safety mechanism the visual identity. Family: office ephemera.
2. **Air-traffic flight strips in a strip bay.** Each open loop is a strip; handled strips leave the bay but are never destroyed; the controller glances, doesn't read everything. Carries "short list of what needs you" plus ambient oversight. Family: operational interfaces.
3. **Pigeonhole mail-sorting case (post office).** Sorting frame with two destinations: the one slot that is yours, and the rest filed by date. Family: physical mail infrastructure.
4. **The "While you were out" message slip pad.** Carbon-copy pink slips: who called, "please call back", "will call again". Maps to Needs reply / Waiting on others / Action required. Family: office ephemera (second member, kept because it carries the categories).
5. **Split-flap departure board.** Count of things still waiting, rows that flip; the morning glance. Family: public information display.
6. **Double-entry ledger with reversing entries.** Kept vs set aside columns; every archive has an equal reversing entry (Undo). Family: accounting notation.
7. **Swiss/International Typographic style signage (wayfinding program).** A strict grid and one directive per sign: "Needs you" / "Set aside". Family: graphic identity programs.

Families: office ephemera (1, 4), operational interfaces (2), physical mail (3), public displays (5), accounting notation (6), graphic identity programs (7). Five families, no family has more than two.
