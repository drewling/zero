# Comparable first-viewport copy

Observed **2026-09-29 UTC**, approximately 20:59–21:03Z. Six real public homepages were opened read-only through Aside in one task. Each quote was checked against its saved screenshot by the copywriter. Images are 2880 × 1800 physical pixels. The browser agent could not set or verify CSS viewport dimensions, so these are desktop-sized first-viewport captures, not a claim of a particular CSS breakpoint. All six loaded without a redirect or access failure. This is category-clarity research, not verification of their performance, privacy or AI claims.

Quotation convention: line breaks collapsed, typographic quotation marks rendered as straight quotes, long dash rendered as `--`. Wording and capitalization otherwise preserved. Original punctuation remains visible in the screenshots.

## 1. SaneBox

- URL: <https://www.sanebox.com/>
- Captured: 2026-09-29 UTC.
- Headline: **Eliminate "Inbox Clutter" with SaneBox AI**
- Subhead: "SaneBox AI Users Spend On Average 3-4 Hours Less Per Week Managing Their Emails."
- CTA: "Try it now for free"
- Evidence: [first viewport](comparables/sanebox-first-viewport.png).
- Why the category is clear: the headline names the familiar inbox problem before naming the technology. The next line stays in the email context rather than inventing a metaphor.
- Transfer to zero: name inbox cleanup first. Do not borrow the time-saving figure, broad reassurance that nothing slips through, or the AI-first label. Those are not zero evidence.

## 2. Superhuman

- URL: <https://superhuman.com/>
- Captured: 2026-09-29 UTC.
- Headline: **Superpowers, everywhere you work**
- Subhead: "Mail, Docs, and AI that works in every app and tab"
- CTA: "Get Superhuman"
- Evidence: [first viewport](comparables/superhuman-first-viewport.png).
- What supplies clarity: the headline alone does not identify an email product. The subhead names Mail and Docs, and the upper banner says "Looking for Superhuman Mail?". This homepage is a suite pitch, not a clean inbox-tool pitch.
- Transfer to zero: treat this as the contrast case. A small unfamiliar product cannot leave its category to a subhead or a decorative panel. Do not import the expansive "everywhere" promise.

## 3. Shortwave

- URL: <https://www.shortwave.com/>
- Captured: 2026-09-29 UTC.
- Headline: **Automate your email with AI**
- Subhead: "Save hours each day with the Shortwave Agent -- organize, schedule, write and search with just a prompt"
- CTA: "Get started for free"
- Evidence: [first viewport](comparables/shortwave-first-viewport.png).
- Why the category is clear: a verb acts on a familiar noun, "email". The subhead names concrete jobs rather than expecting visitors to know what an agent does.
- Transfer to zero: use a clear action and object. Do not copy its many-job scope, schedule claim, prompt interaction or time savings. zero is not being positioned as a replacement mail app or general assistant.

## 4. HEY

- URL: <https://www.hey.com/>
- Captured: 2026-09-29 UTC.
- Headline: **We finally fixed your email + calendar!**
- Subhead: "Gmail, Outlook, and Apple got complacent and took their eye off the ball. Then along came HEY."
- CTA: "TRY HEY FREE FOR 30-DAYS"
- Evidence: [first viewport](comparables/hey-first-viewport.png).
- Why the category is clear: "email + calendar" makes the category unmistakable despite a broad "fixed" promise. Named incumbent products explain its replacement positioning.
- Transfer to zero: name the familiar thing. Do not copy its competitive attack or replacement story. zero keeps the visitor's Gmail/Apple Mail workflow, and has no calendar promise.

## 5. Spark Mail

- URL: <https://sparkmailapp.com/>
- Captured: 2026-09-29 UTC.
- Headline: **Smart. Focused. Email.**
- Subhead: "Fast, cross-platform email designed to filter out the noise - so you can focus on what's important."
- CTAs: "Free download" and "Buy now"
- Evidence: [first viewport](comparables/sparkmail-first-viewport.png).
- Why the category is clear: "Email" is explicit even in a short headline, and the subhead describes filtering rather than an obscure mental model.
- Transfer to zero: the benefit can be short when the category is named. zero should be more concrete about the actual keep/archive action and Mac/Gmail limitations. Do not copy cross-platform availability or its speed claim.

## 6. Clean Email

- URL: <https://clean.email/>
- Captured: 2026-09-29 UTC.
- Headline: **Clean your inbox of emails you don't need. Then keep it clean.**
- Subhead: "Clean Email helps you manage your mailbox overloaded with unread and unwanted emails more efficiently -- while keeping your data safe and respecting your privacy. Clean up emails with our email inbox cleaner app."
- CTA: "Try it Now For Free"
- Evidence: [first viewport](comparables/clean-email-first-viewport.png).
- Why the category is clear: it describes a familiar cleanup task directly. The illustrated receipt, archive, block and keep actions make the operation tangible without asking visitors to decode a theme.
- Transfer to zero: show ordinary email examples and where each goes. Do not import deletion/block/unsubscribe features or an unqualified data-safety claim. zero's recovery and data transfer must be stated concretely.

## Decision carried into the draft

The SaneBox, Shortwave and Clean Email captures independently support a narrow pattern: a visitor can recognize the job when the hero names a familiar email/inbox task. This does not prove conversion lift or a universal best practice. The recommended zero headline is **Clean up your Gmail inbox on your Mac.** The new wording is zero's own, constrained by its Gmail-only Mac app, reversible archives, actual run button and the cold-reader results. Its second section shows a colleague question, newsletter, payment problem and receipt, then the rules, undo and disclosures answer the doubts a reader raised. We intentionally avoid competitors' broad AI, speed, platform, privacy and unattended-operation claims.
