## Diagnose the real weak link

Audience derives from actual product constraints: Gmail users on supported Apple Silicon Macs who want an inbox sorter and can accept a provider key, third-party data and an installer footprint. No invented demographics, market demand, conversion rate, testimonial or customer outcome.

Baseline job clarity is 4/5 in all three model reads, story 3/5, visuals 2/5, readiness 2/2/3. The first weak point is not an obscure headline anymore. It's the repeated demonstration and the confidence gap between a friendly utility and its actual install/data/cost tradeoffs. The approved hero hook is retained. The story must explain agency and recovery. The offer must be an informed install decision, not “free sorting” or an automatic permission to run curl.

## What funnels changed

1. **One actual exchange.** The offer is the next informed step, not a promise of a frictionless/free experience. Cost leads with own Jev key and TypeSafe billing; free AGPL app license follows. The pricing link points to verified model documentation.
2. **Fix the story, not a working hook.** Remove Stays/Archived's second sorting lesson and the decorative Rules chapter in A. Recovery adds information that hero does not show. B tests whether the decision ledger belongs before persuasion.
3. **One acquisition path, not CTA clutter.** Hero/nav Install routes land before ledger warnings and command. Final Copy command is functional. No new repeated Install buttons, fake urgency, signup form, lead magnet, upsell ladder or artificial guarantee.
4. **Earn confidence with facts.** First-run check and actual restore mechanics replace vague “safe” assurance. Security/product tradeoffs remain acceptable reasons to decline.
5. **Plainness beats curiosity here.** The framework's generic curiosity-first suggestion is not adopted. Owner rejected riddles, and category clarity is a hard product requirement. The approved headline says Gmail/inbox/Mac directly.

## What impeccable changed

1. **One text unit, one job.** Each follow-on heading names what that section adds. Requirements stay in hero, rules in one explanation/view, material disclosures in one ledger. Footer/control labels are wayfinding, not repeated story paragraphs.
2. **Resolve real ambiguity.** Draft2 replaces B's odd “Your Gmail account can stay...” with **Keep reading your connected Gmail account in Gmail or Apple Mail**. The account remains Gmail; Apple Mail is the client. A uses the same clarification.
3. **Name the actual data, not internal shorthand.** Draft2 spells out both reply-history fields: whether you sent the latest message and whether you've replied to that sender before. Preview limit is 160 characters, checked in classification source. It does not generalize to the optional draft path.
4. **Scope high-stakes action.** Draft2 changes ambiguous **It never sends automatically** to **Drafts never send automatically**. Sending and model archiving are not conflated. The actual Send reply control remains named.
5. **Explain the warning honestly.** Draft2 names zero's incomplete Google app review, not an unspecified “Google's review”. No reassurance that bypassing a warning is safe, no promise of a completion date.
6. **Make objects add information.** Source-faithful readable Undo rows replace greeked bars. The facsimile remains explicitly illustrative, because a real mailbox screenshot would expose owner mail and there's no demo mode.
7. **Readable not merely short.** Compact labels organize full disclosures. Full-width section geometry still needs capped paragraph measures and 320–2560 responsive checks by designer. Heading type alternatives and ligature proof are pending, not solved by rewriting text alone.
