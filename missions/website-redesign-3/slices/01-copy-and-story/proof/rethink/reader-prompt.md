# Whole-page reader prompt

You are considering installing a Mac utility you have not used before. The attached screenshots show one proposed public landing page at desktop and narrow-phone widths. Some long-page views are also supplied as ordered section crops so you can read the details. The supplied text is the same page's full copy. Illustration rows are made-up examples, not customer outcomes.

Use only these screenshots and words. Do not browse, read files, run commands, call tools or use account or project context. Do not assume the product has capabilities not shown. Do not try to please the author.

Answer:
1. In one sentence, what does the product do and who is it for?
2. What is confusing or contradictory, in the words or the visual objects? Quote exact text or identify the visual moment.
3. Which sections or lines would you skip, and why? Which facts feel repeated?
4. What information is missing before you could decide?
5. What would make you install it, and what would make you leave? Separate product/security tradeoffs from page-presentation problems.
6. Who actually sorts and archives the mail: you by dragging, the app after you start it, or a background process? Say what evidence on the page led you there.
7. Explain how you would undo a mistaken archive and what email data leaves the Mac. If it is unclear, say so rather than inventing an answer.
8. Rate the page from 1 (poor) to 5 (clear) on: job clarity, ease of following the story, usefulness of the visuals, finding safety/data/requirements/cost information, and readiness to make a decision. Explain each rating briefly.

Finish with the three most important presentation changes, in priority order. A product limitation can remain a valid reason not to install even if the page explains it well.
