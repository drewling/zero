# Whole-page structure research

Observed 2026-09-29 around 22:39Z. Public HTML text fetched read-only with `webfetch`; this supplements the six earlier visually checked first-viewport captures. These observations cover wording and document order, not visual layout, conversion results or independent verification of competitor claims. The three added Mac utilities are relevant craft comparables, not a measured ranking of popularity.

## SaneBox

Source: <https://www.sanebox.com/>

Order observed: inbox-clutter promise and trial CTA → compatibility (“Works with every email client”) → named assistant jobs → feature inventory → customer proof → explicit security/privacy block → FAQ → trial CTA.

Useful difference from zero's current six equal chapters: compatibility is a quick suitability answer near the promise; trust is a coherent decision block rather than scattered small reassurances. Its big time-saving, universal-client, Google-verified and never-share claims do not transfer to zero. Its many-job feature inventory is not a model for a one-job utility.

## Clean Email

Source: <https://clean.email/>

Order observed: cleanup promise and CTA → recognizable inbox problems → three-step cleaning/unsubscribe/automation workflow → outcome categories → detailed features/use cases → customer proof → explicit privacy/data-access details → CTA.

Useful: the operational section tells a sequence rather than naming an interface object. Counterexample: the page is long and repeats benefits; a tiny tool need not copy its size. Unsubscribe, deletion, broad provider support and audited-security claims do not transfer. zero's manual run and third-party model data must remain explicit.

## Rectangle, added Mac utility 1

Source: <https://rectangleapp.com/>

Exact category line: “Move and resize windows in macOS using keyboard shortcuts or snap areas”. Order: category → download and compact free/open-source/platform line → two concrete interaction modes (“Shortcut Simplicity”, “Pick your snap areas”) → compatibility/migration detail → support and related products.

Useful: a narrow job earns very few explanatory chapters. Requirement/license information sits with the download decision. Each feature answers a different way to use the tool, not a different decorative window. Do not import its drag interaction into zero, where the visitor does not manually file mail.

## Things, added Mac utility 2

Source: <https://culturedcode.com/things/>

Exact category line: “Things is the award-winning personal task manager that helps you plan your day, manage your projects, and make real progress toward your goals.” Order: category and introduction video → short “Simply Powerful” promise with feature-detail link → install/platform cards → user/editorial evidence → newsletter/footer.

Useful: the main page need not reproduce the entire feature spec. A demonstration and a link to deeper explanation can keep the story small. zero cannot hide material data/cost/install warnings behind a features link or borrow awards/testimonials. The praise visible here is the vendor's selected quotations, not independently assessed sentiment.

## Raycast, added Mac utility 3

Source: <https://www.raycast.com/>

Exact category line: “A collection of powerful productivity tools all within an extendable launcher. Fast, ergonomic and reliable.” Order: launcher promise → keyboard/workflow demonstration → extension examples → AI assistant/tool demonstration → people/social proof → repeated-task jobs → community/developer material → download.

Useful: concrete action examples do explanatory work. Counterexample: the broad ecosystem deserves many sections that zero does not; “everything”, speed/reliability metrics, AI breadth and social proof are not zero claims. For zero, one convincing keep/archive/recovery sequence may be stronger than four different decorative app metaphors.

## Evidence boundaries carried into outline choice

Rectangle and Things independently show that a narrowly framed Mac app can put category and acquisition close together and avoid a large feature list on its main page. SaneBox and Clean Email independently put privacy/access into explicit decision blocks. These are observed patterns, not conversion-tested best practices. The correct zero sequence must also answer the isolated readers' concerns and preserve its less frictionless setup: Apple Silicon/macOS 26, Gmail only, unverified Google app, non-notarized installer, conditional dependencies, own TypeSafe Jev billing and optional drafting-provider data.
