## Two genuinely different story alternatives for joint selection

### A. Action → recovery → decision → install (four blocks)

Keep hero B. Open the archived result next, not another before/after inbox. One readable zero Undo object answers “what if the model is wrong?” while short prose explains rules, starred/uncertain guardrails and Gmail/Apple Mail. One compact decision ledger answers Google access, data recipients, billing, optional drafts and installer footprint. Terminal closes the sequence. Rhythm can be one continuous desktop, not alternating bands.

Advantage: removes two whole explanatory chapters and gives the second visual a new job. Risk: rule explanation could feel crowded around recovery; decision ledger must remain readable at 390. Detailed cost/footprint remain below the hero, but before the actual command. Approved hero CTA links to this informed install area.

### B. Suitability first → choose a rule → recovery → install (five blocks)

Keep hero B. Immediately answer “is this suitable for my Mac and inbox?” with a requirements/access/cost/data ledger. Next show one source-faithful Rules editing view, not invented per-email reason badges, with an editorial explanation of keep/archive decisions. Then a readable Undo proof. Terminal closes. Optional drafting is confined to the ledger, not a new feature pitch.

Advantage: material tradeoffs appear before further product persuasion. Risk: dense trust content interrupts the hero's visual operation and keeps Rules as an additional chapter; a Rules view may still decorate rather than prove classification.

Recommendation is not finalized until design lead weighs the objects and rhythm. Current evidence favors A's nonduplicate proof and one coherent trust block, with B retained as a real risk-first alternative rather than a renamed A.
