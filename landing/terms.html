These are the terms for using zero, a macOS app made by Tayo Onabule (Drewl). Installing or using zero means you're okay with what's below. We've kept it plain.

What zero is

zero is a free, open-source desktop app that reads your Gmail on your Mac, keeps the threads that still need you, and archives the rest reversibly. It uses your Google sign-in, TypeSafe's Jev service for sorting, and any optional drafting provider you configure. We don't host it or run it for you.

What you need

  • A Mac that meets the requirements on the home page.
  • Your own Google account, which you connect through Google's sign-in.
  • A TypeSafe Jev API key for sorting, configured in Settings → Sorting engine. Optional reply drafts use the provider configured in Settings → AI engine. Any cost, retention, or limit is between you and the relevant provider.

You're responsible for keeping your accounts and your machine secure, and for using zero only with accounts you're allowed to use.

Using it responsibly

Don't use zero to break the law, to access mail that isn't yours, or in a way that violates Google's or your AI provider's terms. Don't try to pass zero off as your own product or strip out its license notices.

It comes as-is

It's built to be careful — it never deletes mail and every archive is reversible — but software has bugs, and Gmail and AI providers change under it. You use zero at your own risk, and you're responsible for what lands in your sent folder and what gets archived. Review what it did; keep your own backups of anything important.
